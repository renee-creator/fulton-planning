# TREE

TREE is the school's one app for Fulton Community School & Farm. It grew out of Planning Studio and now holds planning, documentation, children and family conferences, and the calendar. It has six sections, Today, Observations, Plans, Documentation, Children and Calendar, with a Keep an observation button always one tap away. Observations come before Plans and Documentation because they feed both. Each observation shows where it has been used, in a plan, on a slide or on a wall. The Lens keeps the word moment for the passages it quotes from plans and observations.

- **Today** greets the educator and shows what is waiting for them: notes from the director, plans to finish or review, final remarks still to write, words to check on a copied photo, and family conferences coming up. It also shows today's calendar and the latest moments.
- **Plans** opens on the week, and from Saturday on the coming week, the way the Weekly bin does. Each small group's Individual plan sits beside the Classroom plan, with All plans, Threads, Projects and the Lens one tap away. An Individual plan signs up for a space for the small group, Tuesday to Friday from 9 to 10:30 unless the educator changes the days or times, and the sign-up shows on the calendar.
- **Documentation** holds Moments, Slides and Walls. A moment can tag the children in it, and a moment kept without signal waits on the phone and sends itself once back in range. A slide is made from moments, with up to three photos, portrait or landscape, the team's words, the children's words, and DRDP measures printed only when an educator chooses. DRDP measures can also be linked to a moment. TREE suggests levels from documentation, against the DRDP (2025) Infant and Toddler View and Preschool Transitional Kindergarten View of August 20, 2026, and an educator reviews each one. A reviewed level is evidence toward a level, for the educator's DRDP rating in DRDP Online, never a DRDP rating itself, and one piece of documentation is never treated as a level reached. Every family printout that names a level says it is evidence from our documentation, not a DRDP rating, and the conference form's box is named DRDP evidence. A measure shows its strongest reviewed level, with its date, measures follow the instrument's order, the portfolio's page covers this school year, emerging is allowed at a last level only where the instrument allows it, measures that begin above the first level can be marked Not yet, and the conditional health measures are read for every child, optional without an IEP or IFSP.
- **Children** is the roster, kept with the same records Family Conferences used. Each child has a page with their documentation, conferences over time, DRDP evidence and a portfolio for the family. A child who leaves or moves on to kindergarten moves to Former children and nothing is removed. Conferences holds everything the Family Conferences app did, inside TREE. A child's portfolio prints the DRDP within the moments by default. Each level an educator accepted prints as evidence toward that level in a small Learning seen here band on the page of the moment or slide it was read from, so the reading stays with the experience and is seen over time. Beside the Portfolio PDF button, the DRDP can instead print at the end or be left out. Each month's name sits small above its first piece, each page carries its date and the child's age, and the class and educators are on the cover.
- **Calendar** holds everything the Fulton Calendar did, inside TREE, with family conferences shown on the same week. Weeks and months start on Monday. Its own settings are named Calendar settings, and the Conferences page's are Conference settings, so neither is mistaken for TREE's Settings.

A plan or an observation saves only what changed since it was opened, so two educators writing in different sections of the same plan keep each other's words, and a section another educator changed shows fresh while the one being typed in is left alone. The Observations page shows 30 at a time, with Show more, and Settings opens with links to each of its sections. A connection problem reads as a plain sentence on screen, and the technical reason goes to the server log.

The director can see TREE as any educator from Settings, with their name and their class, to check what each class sees. A band above the header says whose view it is, the header names the educator being viewed, TREE opens on Today at the top, nothing is saved while looking, and keeping an observation says so rather than Kept. Back to my view returns to the director's own TREE. Educators sign in with their first name. The director adds educators in Settings, and each new educator, or one whose code was cleared, gets six starting numbers shown there. The educator signs in once with those numbers and then chooses four of their own. Every wrong name or passcode gets the same answer. After five wrong tries a name waits fifteen minutes, then twice as long after each further five, up to a day. Changing a passcode signs out every other device. Record names are set by the server, never by what a page sends. Passcodes set on Render still work.

TREE is published at https://renee-creator.github.io/fulton-planning/ (tree.html is the same page). The old Planning Studio is kept at planning-studio.html. The Family Conferences and Calendar pages inside it are tree-conferences.html and tree-calendar.html, built from the copies in src/embed. Run `python3 tools/build.py` to build all three and index.html.

The calendar's sign-up rules and Google calendar reading came from the Fulton Calendar server and live in calendar.js and calendar-core.js, keeping their records under calendar/ in the records repository. Sign-ins live in access/accounts.json in the records repository and are never sent to a browser except to the director.

## Planning Studio, as it grew

Weekly curriculum planning for Fulton Community School & Farm. Educators complete the Classroom and Individual Curriculum Plans, upload paper plans to have them transcribed, follow threads across weeks, and download printable PDF or Word forms. Threads, projects, the lens, and project stories each download as a PDF and as a Word file, laid out for display with a few things that matter set large, in educators' own words. Every one of these printouts, and every report built the same way, puts each section's heading on its own line above content that uses the full width of the sheet, with margins of about two thirds of an inch, and a heading always stays with its first lines. The tinted statement at the top holds a few lines, and a longer one carries on below it as body text. A curriculum web that cannot fit a branch name or its materials ends the line with an ellipsis. The full project PDF prints every observation it holds. A wonder already written as a question prints as written, without I wonder in front. The Word file follows the PDF. Threads, projects, and the lens each offer a Summary, which is one sheet, and a Full set, which adds a sheet for every thread, every part of a project, or every moment. A run of moments uses the whole width of the sheet, with the week beside the credit, so the narrow column is not left empty. Every sheet stands alone. A group under a heading that would fit a sheet of its own starts a fresh sheet rather than being split, a heading that does carry on is repeated with the word continued, and a page that is barely over is set a little smaller rather than spilling a few lines. A completed curriculum plan prints to the writing it holds, with no ruled lines and no blank space stretched to fill a sheet, so it holds two pages. The first sheet is the observation through the provocations, and the second is the curriculum brainstorm, the web, and the languages, open questions and environment notes. A section left empty prints as its heading alone. Blank forms keep their rules and their room to write. These pages use the same two typefaces as the app, Bricolage Grotesque and Figtree, kept in the fonts folder under the SIL Open Font License and carried inside each PDF and Word file. A short page is set larger so it fills the sheet. No printout names the AI tool. The Lens page reads the year's plans through a lens, this year Graphic Languages, and gathers moments in educators' own words by classroom and week. The school can hold several lenses, added, duplicated and removed by the director in Program settings, and the page shows one at a time. Claude can draft a lens from its name, proposing what it means here, the questions, the languages and the words, which the director uses or discards. Check the words counts what each word of a lens would find across the saved plans and names the sections it found them in, so words that catch planning language rather than children's work can be dropped. That count is arithmetic on the school's own plans, not Claude. Each lens keeps its own readings, and a reading made before the school held more than one opens under the first lens. Every educator types their own name at the top, and a shared sign-in such as Staff is never used as an educator's name. A plan cannot be started or marked ready without one.

Notice and Wonder is the school's observational frame and sits beside the plans an educator can start. A moment is written in two boxes, what I noticed and what I wonder, and it is never filed against a week. It keeps the educator who wrote it, the day, the classroom, and optionally a project or a thread. Nothing expires and nobody approves it. Every educator sees every moment, because supporting children is school-wide. A plan for that classroom offers its moments without opening them, and one tap drops the words into the observation so nothing is written twice. Find threads and the lens read moments alongside plans, and a single moment is never enough on its own to raise a thread. A moment is never counted as a plan, so it stays out of the week packet, out of Final remarks due, and out of any plan download. A moment can also start from a photo, such as chart paper where an educator wrote down what children said in a group conversation or a story they told together. Upload a photo sits beside Keep a moment, and several photos become several moments, opened one after another. The photo is kept in the records beside the moment. Claude copies the writing into What I noticed exactly as written, one line per turn with each child's name, correcting nothing, and marks a name or word it could not read clearly with [?] or [unclear] for the educator to check against the photo. What I wonder is always left for the educator. The day the photo was taken is suggested and can be changed. Each moment prints on its own sheet, with its photo, as a PDF and a Word file, and the Full PDF of the Moments page carries the photos too. Any educator can change a kept moment, its words, day, classroom, project or thread, with Change on the card. The moment keeps the name of the educator who kept it, and the card notes who changed it. Moments live on their own page, Moments at the end of the top menu, which opens on the record rather than on a blank form. Keep a moment is there, and after keeping one an educator lands back on the record with it at the top. The same moments can be read five ways, most recent first, by classroom, by educator, by month, or by project and thread, and the way of looking is remembered. A classroom pill and a range narrow what is in view. Summary PDF prints the view to one sheet for display and Full PDF prints every moment with what the educator wondered, both with a Word copy, and the printout carries the same groups the page is showing. The director can ask Claude to read the moments in view and name up to five patterns, each a short label with the moments it draws on, and that reading stays on the page for the whole team and prints with it. On a phone the one column runs Week of, Start a plan, the page content, and then the rest of the side panels, so an educator reaches the work before the furniture. Feedback from the director and Final remarks due are the two panels that go looking for an educator, so when either holds something a single line sits above everything saying what is waiting, with a tap that scrolls down to it. The line never appears on a wider screen, where those panels are already in view. More than one lens can be marked as this year's, and a moment shows every lens of this year that finds it, by its words or by the languages of expression an educator marked on it. A project page shows How it has grown, everything the project holds in the order it happened, and In the children's words, every line from a child in its moments and plans, with names and dates. The Wall turns a project into a run of sheets that hang in order, each on its own page with a photo, the children's words chosen for it, and a few lines from the team, written by an educator or drafted only from what the team wrote and kept when an educator chooses it. A project holds the moments kept for it and the moments kept for any of its threads, and lists the slides made from them. A moment shows the slides it is on, and a slide shows the threads and projects of its moments. A slide's photo picker opens on the slide's class, with All for every class, and Show more reaches every photo. Threads the team decides to follow further become projects, which belong to a classroom, can be joined by other classrooms, and can also begin school-wide.

Fulton's classes mix, so a moment belongs to its own class and to the class of every child tagged in it. It shows on the card as West and North classes, appears in each of those classes' plans, Moments views, lens and slide picker, and prints with both. A Kaymbu subject can name more than one class, such as North West, and with no class named the children are looked for across the school. Children, at the end of the top menu, is the roster. Its Portfolios view shows each child at Fulton by month of the school year, with the number of moments and slides in the child's portfolio from that month. A number opens the child's documentation, a month with none can be ticked for a page made outside TREE, and past months with nothing are shaded. The ticks are kept on the child's record, where Family Conferences kept them, and the Conferences page no longer has a Portfolios page of its own. It came in once from the Family Conferences records with each child's name, classroom, small group, first day and next year's plan, and from now on the roster is kept here. Each child is At Fulton, Left Fulton or Moved on to kindergarten. A child who leaves moves to Former children with the day they left, and nothing about them is removed. Their record stays until the director chooses how long records are kept. Every change is noted on the child with the day and who made it. Program settings has a Printing card with two switches for the school's name, one for display documentation and one for plans and records, and the starting choice for children's names on display pieces, first names, first name and last initial, initials, or no names. Names change only on the printout. The words in a moment never change. A moment sheet and each sheet of a wall print centred, with the photo taking at least the top two thirds of the page and more when the words are few, the title, then the classroom, the day and the educator under it, and the words set larger or smaller so the sheet holds one page. A wall sheet holds up to three photos, the first chosen the largest, and can be seen as it will print before printing. A moment keeps every word that was written, so a long one carries on to a second sheet. A wall sheet with more words than fit says so before printing. The Word file follows the PDF. The tree-icons folder holds the TREE icon for when the app takes the name TREE.

## Moments from Kaymbu

Moments shared from Kaymbu by email to the TREE address become TREE moments. Select the Moments in Kaymbu, send them to tree@childfamilycommunity.com, and put the class in the subject line, such as South or South Class. The subject can also name a project or a thread, such as South Kindness. Each Moment's title and the educator's note are kept word for word, the photo is kept in the records at the largest size Kaymbu gives, and the moment is credited to the educator who shared it. The children are tagged by the names written in the note, the same way Tag the children named works on the page. When several photos arrive with the same title and note, the children named are offered on each photo instead, and the photo waits on Today as Check who is in a Kaymbu photo, so each photo carries only the children pictured in it. A moment where TREE could not tell the class or the children, where two children share a first name, where a name appears only in the title, or where a child's first name is also an everyday word, such as River, Daisy or Peace, is not tagged outright. It waits on Today as Tag the children on a Kaymbu moment, for that class's educators and the director. Saving it with Change is the check. A Moment sent twice is only kept once. Kaymbu removes the camera's date from its photos, so each moment takes the day its photo reached Kaymbu, which is the day it was taken when the Kaymbu app is used in class. A date in the subject line, such as North 10/14 or North Oct 14, sets the day for the whole batch instead. The day can always be changed on the moment.

The email reaches TREE through a Google Apps Script in the school's Google Workspace mailbox, kept in tools/kaymbu-apps-script.gs with its setup steps. It checks every ten minutes and sends each new Kaymbu email to the server's Kaymbu inbox, which only accepts emails that came from Kaymbu and carry the key saved on Render as `KAYMBU_INBOX_KEY`. An email the server could not finish, such as a photo Kaymbu did not send in time, is tried again on the next check.

## Weekly bin

Plans, then Weekly bin. Each Class receives a standard FEED Sonoma bin, and on Tuesday a small group the Class team chooses opens it. The server reads the list FEED posts ahead of delivery at feedsonoma.com/weekly-bin, every six hours and whenever the page is opened, and keeps each week under bins/ in the records, so the year's record of what came grows by itself. A week is kept as FEED last listed it until the day it arrives. A list can also be added by hand when FEED's page cannot be read. From Saturday through Monday the page opens on the coming bin.

Each item shows its farm and the farm's town, and whether it came before. The towns come from FEED's producer list, checked against CCOF and the farms' own pages, and any educator can correct one under Where the farms are. The team keeps an idea bank of questions to hold and simple ways to prepare the food, which anyone can add to. Claude can suggest some of each from the week's produce and what the class is following, and a suggestion joins the idea bank only when an educator keeps it. Suggestions treat the food as food, prepared, tasted and shared, never as an art or play material, and someone eats it. Anything left goes in the chicken buckets for the chickens and goats at the end of the day, the goats only fruits and vegetables. Each Class chooses which ideas to bring to the table, and those print on the bin sheet with the produce and farms, as a PDF and a Word file. Keep an observation on the page keeps it with that week's bin, and it goes on to plans, threads and documentation like any other. From the day a bin arrives until six days later, the observation form anywhere in TREE shows a From the bin button, one tap to keep the observation with that bin and one more to undo. Moments from Kaymbu go with the bin when the word bin is in the subject line, such as South Bin. On Monday to Wednesday, Today shows the week's bin.

## Produce coming

Today and the Weekly bin show what the kitchen ordered for the week, for every educator, from the order confirmations kept for the grant, as a card named Produce coming. It opens on one line with the number of foods and farms and the day they come, and whether it is open is remembered on the device. The server gives only the food, its farm and the day it comes, never prices, totals, order numbers or the vendor, and leaves out bin lines and boxes, since the bin has its own page. Today shows the coming week from Saturday through Friday, with a link to the Weekly bin. The food is for lunch and snacks, so nothing suggests a separate tasting. Each food shows its farm and town and, when the menu uses it, the day and meal, such as Tue lunch, cheese pizza. Every snack comes with seasonal fruit and every lunch with a salad and seasonal vegetables, as the month's menu notes say, so a food with no named dish shows Fruit with snack, Salad with lunch or Side vegetable with lunch, and an onion or garlic shows Cooked into lunches this week. These are TREE's guess, in muted type. The director answers Side dish, Cooked in, or both, on each food, and the answer replaces the guess for everyone. It is kept on the order line and kept when the order is read again. Where the children met a food before is worked out but not shown for now. The Weekly bin page prints the week as a large type sheet for the classroom wall, This week our food came from.

Make a plan from this bin, on the Weekly bin page, starts an Individual plan, or adds to the class's Classroom plan, for the bin's week and the class chosen on the page. It begins with what came in the bin, the farms, and the ideas the class chose from the idea bank, and the plan shows From the bin with a link back to that week.

## Where our food came from

Settings, then Monthly menus. Beside each month, Farm sheet for families prints What we ate in that month, and where it came from, on one page, as a PDF and a Word file, for Sally's Kitchen Digest. It has the number of farms and how many are in Sonoma County, the menu week by week under a line from the menu notes about fruit, salad and vegetables, with the farms whose food went In the dishes and those On the side, from the director's answers or TREE's guess, matched the same way as Coming for lunch and snack, the farms that fed us in three columns with their towns, the foods the children also met in a FEED bin that month, and a line for the recipe of the month. Classroom display prints the month's main foods in large type, each with its farm and town, chosen by how often they were served, with the number of farms. The family newsletter has an optional From our farms section with the same content in a few sentences. None of these ever shows a price.

## Kitchen

The Kitchen page, for the kitchen and the director, is where the month's menu is made. Each school day, Monday to Friday, has a morning snack, lunch and an afternoon snack, Monday a morning snack only, each with a line for its sides, and No school for a closed day. Typing saves to the month's menu record in the same form as before, so the calendar, Today, the farm sheets and the grant read it as they always have. Past dishes lists every dish ever on a menu, with how often and when it was last served, to find by name and tap or drag into a meal, and the same week last year sits beside it. Fill this month drafts the month from past menus, the same day last year first, then the most served dishes in turn, never the same lunch within about two weeks, Monday's morning snack only, and days the calendar shows closed left empty. The draft shows in lighter type until it is saved, and fruit and vegetable sides are always left for the kitchen to choose by season. The page also holds Produce coming with Side dish and Cooked in, the month's notes, the recipe of the month, the staples, and prints of the lunch menu and snack menu, one landscape page each in the layout families know, as PDF and Word, with the farm sheet for families and the classroom display.

In Settings, Sign-ins, the director can mark a sign-in Kitchen. A kitchen sign-in sees Kitchen, Today and the calendar. Its Today shows Produce coming and the week's menu. The server lets it read and write the menus and the staples, answer Side dish and Cooked in, read the kitchen's week and the month's food lines without prices, and read the calendar, and refuses everything else, children, observations, plans, documentation, the grant, the newsletter and sign-ins included. The farm sheets for a kitchen sign-in are made from those food lines. Each farm sheet, classroom display and newsletter section also counts the range of tastes, the different fruits and vegetables from the month's orders and the different dishes on the menu, and the sheet lists the fruits and vegetables.

## Monthly menus

Settings, then Monthly menus, for the director. Add the month's snack and lunch menus as PDFs or photos. Claude reads each one into days, the morning and afternoon snack and the lunch, along with the note about what is served with every meal. The director checks every day against the menu before saving, and nothing shows until then. Saved menus are kept under menus/ in the records, one file a month, and show on the calendar as Snack and lunch, and so on Today. Each meal is its own entry, the morning snack, lunch, and the afternoon snack after lunch, at the times kept with the month's menu, which carry over from the month before and can be changed in the card. Monday has the morning snack only. Months can also come from Sally's Kitchen Digest. A menus record holding only the digest's two Kaymbu links under intake is opened by the server (menu-intake.js), each PDF is read by Claude with the same instructions as Settings, and days the director already wrote are never replaced. The status page shows any month still waiting. When Sally's digest reaches the TREE mailbox, by the Google admin routing rule or forwarded by anyone at the school, the mailbox script (checkKitchenDigest) sends it on and the month is read the same way, from the digest's heading or, without one, from when it was sent. A digest is known by its subject, by Sally's Kaymbu sender, or by attachments labelled lunch, snack or menu, so a new subject line still comes through. The two PDFs are kept with the photos and open from Print the lunch menu and Print the snack menu on each Snack and lunch entry in the calendar, on Today, and in Settings under Monthly menus. Months read before the PDFs were kept get them on the server's next pass. A month can be checked or changed again at any time. Only the director adds or changes menus, and the server enforces it.

## Staff birthdays

Settings, then Staff birthdays, for the director. Each staff member's birthday is kept with the month and day only, in settings/staffbirthdays.json in the records, and shows on the calendar under Birthdays every year, and so on Today, as the name's birthday with Staff birthday beneath. No age is shown. Like the children's birthdays, they stay out of the newsletter.

## Farm to School grant

Documentation, then Farm to School, for the director only. The page shows one quarter, one year from July to June, the way the grant term and the school year run, or the whole grant from the term's start (or the first purchase) to its end. A year or the whole grant gathers every figure the same way and lists each quarter's check-in to read, for an annual or final report, and its summary prints like a quarter's. The server refuses grant records to anyone else. It gathers what TREE already holds, by quarter, against the eight objectives of the CDFA Farm to School Incubator grant. The whole grant runs from the day in Grant details when the term began, and a short line asks for that day only while it is empty.

The page opens with a row of key figures, California food bought, meals and education against their budgets, producers, the share from Sonoma County and school days. The eight objectives follow as a short table, each with its figure and its pace or count, and the official wording one tap away. Every other section is a card whose opening line shows its main figure, and long lists, such as producers, farms, observations found and the family log, show the first few with Show all for the rest. A producer's marks wrap onto their own line, and what they rest on folds under About this farm, so the page fits a phone.

Each summary, quarter, year or whole grant, opens with the same key figures, then the objectives as a compact table with the official wording in small type under it, producers and the farms behind the bins in two columns of farm, town and amount, sorted by amount, and the family log with the date beside each entry.

- **California food bought, objectives 1 to 4.** Order emails and invoices forwarded to the TREE mailbox, from any vendor and any time in the grant, become farm-level purchase records, each item with its producer, unit, price and quantity. Emails and PDF invoices forwarded as attachments are opened too, and a saved email or PDF can be added on the page. FEED's confirmations and invoices are read exactly. Any other vendor's is read by Claude and waits under Orders to check until the director marks it right, and it shows as to check on the worksheet until then. An order with no date it could read takes the day it was kept and waits to be checked. When an order already marked right arrives again with different lines, the lines kept are not replaced. The new reading waits under Orders to check beside them, with Use this reading and Keep what is there. Shipping notices and other mail are recognised and left out. The mailbox script's setup is in tools/kaymbu-apps-script.gs. Bins count as food for student education (1b) and everything else as food for meals and snacks (1a), changeable line by line. TREE asks about a line it is unsure of, under Meals or education? on the page and in a line on the director's Today. A line is unsure when the menu, with its note about fruit at every snack and a salad and vegetables at every lunch, does not use it in the days after it came, or when its food is in a thread, project or team inquiry the team is following, and the card says which. Until the director chooses, the line counts for meals and the procurement worksheet marks it to check. A choice is kept, and a later reading of the same order keeps it.

The farm sheet for families lists cheese, beans and rice, flour and rice cakes once, under Our staples, from a list the director keeps in Settings under Monthly menus, and the weeks show the seasonal food. Purchases from other producers, such as Petaluma Creamery or Next Generation Foods, can be added by hand. Each producer is marked once as small to midsize, BIPOC, female, veteran or limited resource, climate smart, or organic, and lines FEED marks OG count as organic on their own. Procurement worksheet downloads every line, with the farms behind the bins, for the CDFA food procurement worksheet.
- **Farms behind the FEED bins.** A bin is invoiced as FEED Cooperative, so the farms FEED listed for each bin are shown for farm-level data.
- **From the lunch menus.** How many lunches had cheese from Petaluma Creamery and beans or rice from Next Generation Foods.
- **Hands-on food education, objectives 5 to 7.** Observations and plans found by food words educators wrote, such as fruit, cooking or harvest, never by art or play words. Each one shows Counts and Does not count, and only those the director marks Counts are counted on the page, in the objectives and in every summary. The choices are kept in the director's own grant records. Observations kept for a team inquiry are left out.
- **Families, objective 8.** A short log of family meetings, parent participation and foods families brought.
- **Quarterly check-in.** Claude drafts progress, successes and challenges from only the figures and educators' words on the page, and the director rewrites it. Quarter summary prints as a PDF and a Word file.

## What families don't have to provide

On the Farm to School page, an estimate of the groceries and time each family is spared, for the chosen quarter, year or whole grant. School days come from the monthly menus, a full day being lunch and two snacks and a Monday or short day lunch and one snack, and enrollment from Grant details or the children in TREE. Groceries use USDA's food plans for ages 2 to 5, thrifty to liberal, with lunch and two snacks taken as 40 to 50 percent of a day's food and 25 percent added at the high end for organic and local food. Time is 10 to 25 minutes a school day, valued at the Santa Rosa minimum wage. The federal child care food program's rate for the same meals is shown for comparison and never added. Sources are linked on the page, and the year and grant summaries print the figures.

## The grant's eight objectives

The Farm to School page opens with the agreement's eight objectives from the scope of work (draft of March 20, 2025), folded under its heading. Each gives the planned activity, who carries it out, and where on the page its evidence is gathered. Each goal shows where it stands for the chosen period, from the same figures the page gathers. Goal 1 adds the pace needed each month to use the rest of the food budget by the end of the term, against the last three months. Goal 4 counts certified organic and local farms using sustainable practices without certification separately, as the scope of work allows. The people are as they are now, Renee and Destiny for purchasing and Renee with the educators for food education. Every summary prints where each goal stands.

## Local food, how far it travels and where the dollars go

On the Farm to School page, each purchase is one of three kinds. California farms and food hubs are what the grant counts. A specialty supplier, such as Central Milling for organic whole wheat flour, is chosen for quality and kept outside the California totals. A large retailer, such as Trader Joe's or Costco, is recognised by name, or chosen when a purchase is added by hand. How far our food travels gives straight line miles from each farm's town to the school, weighted by what was spent, the share from Sonoma County and within 50 miles, and dollars by county. A FEED bin counts the farms FEED listed for it that week, for its miles and for its counties, each farm taking an even share of the bin. Where the food dollars went shows California farms and food hubs, Sonoma County, specialty suppliers and large retailers, with the share at large retailers by quarter once any are kept, and the statewide context from the 2026 California Farm to School Evaluation, cited as context and never as a measure of this school's spending.

## Small copies of photos

Cards and lists show a small copy of each photo, about 640 pixels on its longest side, so a page of moments loads quickly on a phone. Slides, walls, portfolios, every printout and a photo opened on its own use the original, which is never changed or replaced. A photo kept in TREE gets its small copy as it is kept, a photo from Kaymbu keeps Kaymbu's own smaller copy, and photos kept before small copies existed get one from the director's browser, a few at a time, while TREE is open.

## Plans that begin from evidence

A plan can begin from a pattern in What these observations keep returning to, from a thread, or from a project. A project asks which of its threads the plan continues, or the project as a whole. Whatever the plan began from stays beside it in The evidence panel while it is written, with the project's reading and intent, the thread's question and last remarks, lines from the plans that followed the thread, and every observation with its photo and the children's words. Plans that follow a thread or took in observations show the same panel. It never prints.

Only the director changes a project. Educators read it, add a thread to it, keep observations for it, and start plans from it. The server refuses any other change to a project from anyone else.

## Team inquiries

Plans, then Inquiries. A team inquiry is where the team thinks through a question about children's development and its own practice, such as a child running from a request, kept beside the curriculum plans so a plan never becomes about a behavior. Everyone reads them. The director starts and writes each one with the team, the way a project works. Educators add observations and threads to it, write what happened when the team tried something, and start plans from it, and the server refuses any other change from them. Each belongs to one class or to the whole school. The director can draft any section from the inquiry's evidence, or strengthen the wording, using only what educators wrote.

An inquiry holds what we see, when and where it happens, readings of what it might be telling us (set aside, never deleted, so the change in thinking stays visible), what we will try, what we will watch for, a date to look again, and each time the team looked again. It can be read through any lens, with a place to answer the lens questions.

It takes in its own observations, every observation and plan of a connected thread, and connected projects. It gives back on each of them. Observations show the inquiry they are kept for, threads open one, projects list the inquiries alongside them, the Lens lists the inquiries read through it, Today shows an inquiry on its look-again date, and a plan started from an inquiry keeps it in The evidence panel, with the things to try that belong in the curriculum. Observations kept for an inquiry stay out of portfolios and walls. Prints come as a summary and a full PDF, each with Word.

## How it runs

- **Web page** `index.html`, published with GitHub Pages at https://renee-creator.github.io/fulton-planning/
- **Server** `server.js`, running on Render at https://fulton-planning.onrender.com. Open that address in a browser to see a status page.
- **Plans**, followed threads and projects are kept as files in the private repository `fulton-planning-records`. Every change is saved there with the educator's name, so earlier versions can always be recovered from GitHub's history.

This repository holds code only. It never holds plans, children's names, or keys.

## Server settings on Render

| Setting | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `TEACHER_PASSCODES` | Each educator's name and passcode, separated by commas, like `Hannah=maple garden 42,Chris=river stone 7`. Passcodes need at least 6 characters. Removing an educator signs them out. |
| `DIRECTOR_NAMES` | Names from `TEACHER_PASSCODES` who may change Program settings and delete plans or followed threads. Defaults to `Renee`. |
| `GITHUB_TOKEN` | A fine-grained GitHub token with Contents read and write on `fulton-planning-records` only |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | The same key TREE uses |
| `RECORDS_REPO` | Optional. Defaults to `renee-creator/fulton-planning-records` |
| `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` | Optional. Extra websites allowed to use the server |
| `KAYMBU_INBOX_KEY` | Optional. A long secret shared with the Kaymbu Apps Script. Turns on Moments from Kaymbu |
| `READS_PER_HOUR`, `CAREFUL_PER_HOUR` | Optional. Hourly Claude request limits, 800 and 400 by default, sized for a whole-school scan |

## Who can do what

Every educator can write, edit, upload, and download plans, add educator names, and use Claude assist. Any educator can stop following a thread, which moves it to No longer following, and anyone can follow it again. A thread followed by mistake can be undone, which puts it back among the threads Claude found as if it had never been followed. Any educator can connect a plan to a thread the school is following, or disconnect it. A connected plan shows a Following the thread note at the top, and the thread's name prints on the PDF and Word form. A plan is also connected when it was started from the thread or when the thread quotes it. Any educator can delete a plan. The page asks twice before it does, and every deletion is written to the Render log with the educator's name. Any educator can change a followed thread's name, question or classroom, and a project's home classroom, where the old home stays on as a classroom that took part. Any educator can start a project from a followed thread, start a school-wide project, add threads and classrooms to a project, write its stories, and close it. A thread shows what children keep returning to. A project is the team's reading of what lies underneath. Starting one records the experience it grew from, what may lie underneath, the educators' intent, and who decided. The reading and the intent can be revised, and earlier versions are kept with their dates. A project holds possible directions as hypotheses and has no end date or steps to complete. A direction is named with a short label and its materials sit under it, and the drawing sets text smaller until it fits rather than dropping the end of a line. Claude never starts a project, adds a thread to one, or writes the team's reading of what may lie underneath. It points out a new thread that may be related. On the project page it offers the same two kinds of help a plan has. Strengthen wording rewords what educators wrote and adds nothing, and the possible directions can be drafted from what the project and its threads already say. Each suggestion is shown first and nothing changes until an educator chooses it. Every kind of plan that follows a thread stays connected to it. The plan offers the thread's moments first, from any class, with the class's other moments one tap away, and the note at the top shows the thread's question, the weeks it has been seen and its latest evidence. A plan started from a thread, Individual or Classroom, opens its observation with the last reflection on the thread, taken from its most recent complete plan. A plan's Final remarks, or After the week, join the evidence of each thread it follows, and leave a thread it is disconnected from. Drafting from notes, Strengthen wording, the interests draft and the curriculum web all read the thread's question, moments and last reflection, in educators' own words. A followed thread can be brought into the Classroom Plan for the week. If that classroom already has a Classroom Plan for the week the thread is connected to it, and if it has none one is started with nothing written in. The curriculum web draft also reads the threads a plan follows. Only the director writes Director feedback on a plan. Educators see it read-only, are told in the sidebar when a note is waiting, and can reply under it. Everyone can open the Lens page. Only the director can add a child to the roster or remove one, and the server refuses either from anyone else. Educators keep a child's details current. Only the director can run Find threads with Claude, ask Claude to read through the lens, change the lens wording in Program settings, change Program settings, remove a thread permanently, and remove a project. The server enforces this, not just the page.

## Claude assist

Drafting from notes, Strengthen wording, family updates and transcribing everyday plans use Claude Haiku. Sorting scanned pages, finding threads, reading through the lens, project stories and transcribing handwritten scans use Claude Sonnet. The lens reading is told to read for children's thinking, never to rate how a product looks, and to leave an answer empty when the plans say nothing. Every Claude request the server turns away is written to the Render log with the reason. Plans are sent to Anthropic only to answer each request, and Anthropic does not use them to train its AI.

## Editing the page

The page is built from two parts in `src`. `app.html` is Planning Studio and `shim.html` adds sign-in and the connection to the server. After changing either one, run `python3 tools/build.py` to rebuild `index.html`.

## Free plan notes

The Render server sleeps after 15 quiet minutes. The first visit after that takes about a minute while it wakes, and the page says so. Typing is gathered and saved to GitHub within about 15 seconds.
