# TREE

TREE is the school's one app for Fulton Community School & Farm. It grew out of Planning Studio and now holds planning, documentation, children and family conferences, and the calendar. It has six sections, Today, Observations, Plans, Documentation, Children and Calendar, with a Keep an observation button always one tap away. Observations come before Plans and Documentation because they feed both. Each observation shows where it has been used, in a plan, on a slide or on a wall. The Lens calls what it quotes from plans and observations passages.

- **Today** greets the educator and shows what is waiting for them. That is notes from the director, plans to finish or review, final remarks still to write, words to check on a copied photo, and family conferences coming up. It also shows today's calendar and the latest observations, where one Kaymbu post kept once for each child in it shows once, with every child named. The number beside Waiting for you is the sum of what its rows say, and the director also sees the possible food education records to count for the grant. Write my plan hides while that plan is waiting, and never shows for the office or the director. The round Keep an observation button is the one way to keep an observation, on every page, and from the Weekly bin page it keeps the observation with that week's bin. On a phone the observation form folds the languages, the day, a project and a thread under More, and Keep stays in view. A section page has the tab bar to move around, and Back shows only on a page opened from somewhere, a plan, a child, a slide or a project.
- **Plans** opens on the week, and from Saturday on the coming week, the way the Weekly bin does. Each small group's Individual plan sits beside the Classroom plan, with All plans, Threads, Projects and the Lens one tap away. An Individual plan signs up for a space for the small group, Tuesday to Friday from 9 to 10:30 unless the educator changes the days or times, and the sign-up shows on the calendar.
- **Documentation** holds Observations, Slides and Walls. An observation can tag the children in it, and an observation kept without signal waits on the phone and sends itself once back in range. A slide is made from observations, with up to three photos, portrait or landscape, the team's words, the children's words, and DRDP measures printed only when an educator chooses. DRDP measures can also be linked to an observation. TREE suggests levels from documentation, against the DRDP (2025) Infant and Toddler View and Preschool Transitional Kindergarten View of August 20, 2026, and an educator reviews each one. A reviewed level shows as the level itself, with the moment it was read from. The conference form's box is named DRDP evidence. A child's view follows age, with nothing to choose when a child turns three. A measure shows its strongest reviewed level, with its date, measures follow the instrument's order, the portfolio's page covers this school year, emerging is allowed at a last level only where the instrument allows it, measures that begin above the first level can be marked Not yet, and the conditional health measures are read for every child, optional without an IEP or IFSP.
- **Children** is the roster, kept with the same records Family Conferences used. Each child has a page with their documentation, conferences over time, DRDP evidence and a portfolio for the family. A child who leaves or moves on to kindergarten moves to Former children and nothing is removed. Conferences holds everything the Family Conferences app did, inside TREE. A child's portfolio prints the DRDP within the moments by default. Each level an educator accepted prints as that level in a small Learning seen here band on the page of the observation or slide it was read from, so the reading stays with the experience and is seen over time. Beside the Portfolio PDF button, the DRDP can instead print at the end or be left out. Each month's name sits small above its first piece, each page carries its date and the child's age, and the class and educators are on the cover.
- **Share**, in the director's view only, makes a post for the school's Instagram from a project, a project's wall, a thread, a lens, the last two weeks of observations or the weekly bin. A thread's post carries its question as the educators' hypothesis. A lens gathers this school year's observations that name one of its languages or words, and its caption is drafted with the lens's meaning and questions. A food post, for this week, last week, this month or last month, adds a picture of what children ate from the menus and a picture of where the food came from, naming the farms and producers from the weekly bins and the orders with their towns and miles, and never a price. A photo set aside, or any chosen photo, can have its faces softened. A small face finder kept in vendor/faceapi (face-api by Vladimir Mandic, MIT License) runs on the device, so the photo is never sent anywhere for it, and the director taps to add or clear a soft spot, which also covers a written name. Observations kept with a team inquiry are never offered. TREE asks Claude to look at each photo first and sets aside any where a face or a written name may show, and the director can still include one after a warning. Children's names and children's own words are taken out before the caption is drafted, and DRDP never goes into a post. A photo keeps its words over it. A few words on each photo make the children's learning and thinking visible, drawn from the educators' documentation, and beneath them the DRDP domain and the skill, never a code or a level. A thread, project, wall or lens also offers pictures of words only, from the educators' own writing, such as the question being followed, so a post can be made with or without photos. The post ends with a quote from the full Documentation Slides collection, the one Draft finds fits best, on white in TREE's own look, with a photo from the post above it only when the director chooses one. Pictures are 1080 by 1350, saved to Photos or sent straight to Instagram from a phone, with a Word copy of the pictures and caption. Every post is kept in the records under posts, the director's only, as it is made, with its photos, softening, words, caption and quote, so several can be made ahead and posted later from any device. A post is a Draft, Ready to post or Posted, with the day it was posted, and a photo already in a posted post shows Posted before. The finished pictures are kept with the post in Google Drive, so a post opens with them and nothing is drawn again unless something changed. Teaching posts are words only, labeled For families, For educators or For families and educators, and drawn from a newsletter topic, the ideas under a project, a lens, the director's own points, or what the team came to understand in a team inquiry. An inquiry's post carries only that general understanding, why the behavior is developmentally expected even when it is not yet socially acceptable and how adults respond, never the child, the class or the moment, and its observations are never offered as photos. Educators who reach the page go to Today, and the server refuses Share's Claude requests from anyone but the director.
- **Calendar** holds everything the Fulton Calendar did, inside TREE, with family conferences shown on the same week. On every calendar a conference reads only Conference, the child's name and Class, such as Conference, Wren, North. Each slot from the conference sign-up calendar is matched to the conference booked for its time in TREE and reads that way, and a slot nobody has booked reads Open conference time. Children's birthdays stay on every calendar, the kitchen's too. Weeks and months start on Monday. Its own settings are named Calendar settings, and the Conferences page's are Conference settings, so neither is mistaken for TREE's Settings.

A plan or an observation saves only what changed since it was opened, so two educators writing in different sections of the same plan keep each other's words, and a section another educator changed shows fresh while the one being typed in is left alone. The Observations page shows 30 at a time, with Show more, and Settings opens with links to each of its sections. A connection problem reads as a plain sentence on screen, and the technical reason goes to the server log. Dates are written in full, such as November 2, 2026, and times are plain clock times, such as 1:15 pm and 9:00 am, on screen, on the calendar, in reminders, prints and Word files, all from one function kept in clock-time.js and copied into each page.

The director can see TREE as any educator from Settings, with their name and their class, to check what each class sees. A band above the header says whose view it is, the header names the educator being viewed, TREE opens on Today at the top, nothing is saved while looking, and keeping an observation says so rather than Kept. Back to my view returns to the director's own TREE. Educators sign in with their first name. The director adds educators in Settings. The first time, an educator types their first name with the passcode left empty and chooses four numbers. The director sees the codes and can clear one so it is chosen again. Every wrong name or passcode gets the same answer. After five wrong tries a name waits fifteen minutes, then twice as long after each further five, up to a day. Changing a passcode signs out every other device. Record names are set by the server, never by what a page sends. Passcodes set on Render still work.

When an educator leaves, the director chooses Leaving Fulton beside their name in Settings, Sign-ins, and then Today or their last day. Their sign-in ends at the end of that day. From the next day they hear no reminders, leave the check-in schedule, and come off the small group leads and the names offered on plans. Their observations, plans and documentation keep their name as written. Their records page, Growth Plan, journal, check-ins, Proud Moments and evaluations move to Former educators, in Educators under People, which only the director opens. Leaving Fulton shows for anyone with records, any file under staff/ for them or any observation, plan or documentation they wrote. Remove stays only for a sign-in added by mistake with no records, and the server refuses it for anyone else. Until the last day has passed, Staying at Fulton takes the day away. Bring back, in Former educators or in Sign-ins, returns the educator with all their history and a new code to choose at their next sign-in, as after a cleared code, and puts them back on the educators list and as lead of the small groups they led, where nobody else has taken them. The account keeps the last day as leaveOn and the educator's profile keeps it as leftOn.

Every PDF TREE makes opens first in a preview, drawn page by page on the device, with Download PDF, Word where the printout has a Word twin, and Close, so a file is saved only after it has been looked at. Word files and pictures save directly.

Team inquiries are rooted in the school's relationship-based, positive discipline, as the Family Handbook's Behavior and Discipline policy sets out. Each inquiry opens with that line, and has Our relationship with the child and Working with the family, where conversations with the family are kept with their dates and a family conference is marked. What we will try covers connection, changes the adults make and guiding with the children through meeting time and peer problem solving, never a time-out or a punishment, and What we will watch for includes signs the child is managing on their own. When what the team sees describes something physically or emotionally dangerous, the page reminds the team that the policy calls for a conference with the family. Every word TREE drafts for an inquiry, or for a teaching post from one, follows the policy.

TREE is published at https://renee-creator.github.io/fulton-planning/ (tree.html is the same page). The old Planning Studio address, planning-studio.html, now only sends everyone to TREE, and the page no longer holds any code that reaches the server. The Family Conferences and Calendar pages inside it are tree-conferences.html and tree-calendar.html, built from the copies in src/embed. Run `python3 tools/build.py` to build all three and index.html.

The calendar's sign-up rules and Google calendar reading came from the Fulton Calendar server and live in calendar.js and calendar-core.js, keeping their records under calendar/ in the records repository. Sign-ins live in access/accounts.json in the records repository and are never sent to a browser except to the director.

## Planning Studio, as it grew

Weekly curriculum planning for Fulton Community School & Farm. Educators complete the Classroom and Individual Curriculum Plans, upload paper plans to have them transcribed, follow threads across weeks, and download printable PDF or Word forms. Threads, projects, the lens, and project stories each download as a PDF and as a Word file, laid out for display with a few things that matter set large, in educators' own words. Every one of these printouts, and every report built the same way, puts each section's heading on its own line above content that uses the full width of the sheet, with margins of about two thirds of an inch, and a heading always stays with its first lines. The tinted statement at the top holds a few lines, and a longer one carries on below it as body text. A curriculum web that cannot fit a branch name or its materials ends the line with an ellipsis. The full project PDF prints every observation it holds. A wonder already written as a question prints as written, without I wonder in front. The Word file follows the PDF. Threads, projects, and the lens each offer a Summary, which is one sheet, and a Full set, which adds a sheet for every thread, every part of a project, or every observation. A run of observations uses the whole width of the sheet, with the week beside the credit, so the narrow column is not left empty. Every sheet stands alone. A group under a heading that would fit a sheet of its own starts a fresh sheet rather than being split, a heading that does carry on is repeated with the word continued, and a page that is barely over is set a little smaller rather than spilling a few lines. A completed curriculum plan prints only what was written, each box as tall as its words, with no ruled lines, and leaves out a section, a column, a curriculum web or a team line left empty. Plans, blank forms, the week's packet, conference forms and the newsletter print in Andika like every other page. A slide deck leaves out a slide with no title, words or photo, and the Slides page offers to remove such slides. A printed project story leaves out the sentences that only ask the team to add something, and a section with nothing else. The patterns read from observations end at a sentence, never part way through a word. The first sheet is the observation through the provocations, and the second is the curriculum brainstorm, the web, and the languages, open questions and environment notes. A section left empty prints as its heading alone. Blank forms keep their rules and their room to write. Every page and every print, PDF and Word, uses one typeface, Andika, made by SIL for children learning to read. It has a single-story a and g, and a capital I and lowercase l that cannot be confused. Headings are Andika Bold and the words Andika Regular. The files are kept unchanged in the fonts folder with their SIL Open Font License, and each PDF and Word file carries them inside, so a print looks the same on any computer. Conference forms, plans and the newsletter use it too. A short page is set larger so it fills the sheet. No printout names the AI tool. The Lens page reads the year's plans through a lens, this year Graphic Languages, and gathers passages in educators' own words by classroom and week. The school can hold several lenses, added, duplicated and removed by the director in Program settings, and the page shows one at a time. Claude can draft a lens from its name, proposing what it means here, the questions, the languages and the words, which the director uses or discards. Check the words counts what each word of a lens would find across the saved plans and names the sections it found them in, so words that catch planning language rather than children's work can be dropped. That count is arithmetic on the school's own plans, not Claude. Each lens keeps its own readings, and a reading made before the school held more than one opens under the first lens. Every educator types their own name at the top, and a shared sign-in such as Staff is never used as an educator's name. A plan cannot be started or marked ready without one.

Observations follow the school's observational frame, Notice and Wonder, and sit beside the plans an educator can start. Every button and print calls them observations. An observation is written in two boxes, what I noticed and what I wonder, and it is never filed against a week. It keeps the educator who wrote it, the day, the classroom, and optionally a project or a thread. Nothing expires and nobody approves it. Every educator sees every observation, because supporting children is school-wide. A plan for that classroom offers its observations without opening them, and one tap drops the words into the observation so nothing is written twice. Find threads and the lens read observations alongside plans, and a single observation is never enough on its own to raise a thread. An observation is never counted as a plan, so it stays out of the week packet, out of Final remarks due, and out of any plan download. An observation can also start from a photo, such as chart paper where an educator wrote down what children said in a group conversation or a story they told together. Upload a photo sits beside Keep an observation, and several photos become several observations, opened one after another. The photo is kept in the records beside the observation. Claude copies the writing into What I noticed exactly as written, one line per turn with each child's name, correcting nothing, and marks a name or word it could not read clearly with [?] or [unclear] for the educator to check against the photo. What I wonder is always left for the educator. The day the photo was taken is suggested and can be changed. Each observation prints on its own sheet, with its photo, as a PDF and a Word file, and the Full PDF of the Observations page carries the photos too. Any educator can change a kept observation, its words, day, classroom, project or thread, with Change on the card. The observation keeps the name of the educator who kept it, and the card notes who changed it. Observations live on their own page, Observations at the end of the top menu, which opens on the record rather than on a blank form. Keep an observation is there, and after keeping one an educator lands back on the record with it at the top. The same observations can be read five ways, most recent first, by classroom, by educator, by month, or by project and thread, and the way of looking is remembered. A classroom pill and a range narrow what is in view. Summary PDF prints the view to one sheet for display and Full PDF prints every observation with what the educator wondered, both with a Word copy, and the printout carries the same groups the page is showing. The director can ask Claude to read the observations in view and name up to five patterns, each a short label with the observations it draws on, and that reading stays on the page for the whole team and prints with it. On a phone the one column runs Week of, Start a plan, the page content, and then the rest of the side panels, so an educator reaches the work before the furniture. Feedback from the director and Final remarks due are the two panels that go looking for an educator, so when either holds something a single line sits above everything saying what is waiting, with a tap that scrolls down to it. The line never appears on a wider screen, where those panels are already in view. More than one lens can be marked as this year's, and an observation shows every lens of this year that finds it, by its words or by the languages of expression an educator marked on it. A project page shows How it has grown, everything the project holds in the order it happened, and In the children's words, every line from a child in its observations and plans, with names and dates. The Wall turns a project into a run of sheets that hang in order, each on its own page with a photo, the children's words chosen for it, and a few lines from the team, written by an educator or drafted only from what the team wrote and kept when an educator chooses it. A project holds the observations kept for it and the observations kept for any of its threads, and lists the slides made from them. An observation shows the slides it is on, and a slide shows the threads and projects of its observations. A slide's photo picker opens on the slide's class, with All for every class, and Show more reaches every photo. Threads the team decides to follow further become projects, which belong to a classroom, can be joined by other classrooms, and can also begin school-wide.

An observation can carry a small group and the curriculum plan it was kept for, both under More on the observation form. The plans offered are the Individual and Classroom plans of the observation's class from the week before to the week after it, and choosing a plan for a small group fills in that group. The observation shows in that plan's evidence and in By small group on the Observations page, and its card names the group and the plan. Fulton's classes mix, so an observation belongs to its own class and to the class of every child tagged in it. It shows on the card as West and North classes, appears in each of those classes' plans, Observations views, lens and slide picker, and prints with both. A Kaymbu subject can name more than one class, such as North West, and with no class named the children are looked for across the school. Children, at the end of the top menu, is the roster. Its Portfolios view shows each child at Fulton by month of the school year, with the number of observations and slides in the child's portfolio from that month. A number opens the child's documentation, a month with none can be ticked for a page made outside TREE, and past months with nothing are shaded. The ticks are kept on the child's record, where Family Conferences kept them, and the Conferences page no longer has a Portfolios page of its own. It came in once from the Family Conferences records with each child's name, classroom, small group, first day and next year's plan, and from now on the roster is kept here. Each child is At Fulton, Left Fulton or Moved on to kindergarten. A child who leaves moves to Former children with the day they left, and nothing about them is removed. Their record stays until the director chooses how long records are kept. Every change is noted on the child with the day and who made it. Move children, on the Children page, is the director's way to move several children at once. She picks children by Class, small group or next year, ticks the ones to move, and chooses the new Class and small group. Ticks stay while she picks by something else. One review shows every child's change, from where and to where, before anything is saved, and each move is noted on the child exactly as moving one child from the Details tab notes it. A child keeps the same record, so every observation, plan, conference and portfolio stays with them. Moving one child is still done on that child's Details tab. Program settings has a Printing card with two switches for the school's name, one for display documentation and one for plans and records, and the starting choice for children's names on display pieces, first names, first name and last initial, initials, or no names. Names change only on the printout. The words in an observation never change. An observation sheet and each sheet of a wall print centred, with the photo taking at least the top two thirds of the page and more when the words are few, the title, then the classroom, the day and the educator under it, and the words set larger or smaller so the sheet holds one page. A wall sheet holds up to three photos, the first chosen the largest, and can be seen as it will print before printing. An observation keeps every word that was written, so a long one carries on to a second sheet. A wall sheet with more words than fit says so before printing. The Word file follows the PDF. The tree-icons folder holds the TREE icon for when the app takes the name TREE.

Several photos chosen together ask whether they are one observation or one each. One observation is written once, and the educator taps the children in each photo. Each photo is kept as its own record with the shared words and a shared group, so each child's portfolio, conference evidence and DRDP evidence get only the photos they are in, while the card shows the photos together. A slide made from it takes the photo chosen and any other whose children are all on the slide. Changing one changes the words, day, class, project and thread of all of them, and each photo keeps its own children. Change also has Add a photo. On an observation without a photo it becomes the photo, and on one with a photo it joins the same observation as another photo, with its own children. Only the first photo is read for written words. The others are stored with it.

An observation card says which threads it belongs to, as In the thread and the thread's name. The line is read from each thread, including the observations Find threads gave it, and from any photo in the observation's group, so nothing is written to the observation and taking it out of a thread takes the line away.

## A thread an observation might belong to

When an educator keeps an observation, or a Kaymbu post arrives, the server waits until the words have been quiet for a moment and asks the quick model whether they clearly fit a thread the team is following. A photo alone, a word or two, an observation a team inquiry holds, and threads it is already in are never asked about. The answer is kept on the observation, and on every photo of a Kaymbu post, as a suggestion. The card, Change and Today say Might belong in the thread and its name, with Add it and Not this. Add it puts the observation into the thread the way Find threads does, as evidence held by the thread, so the thread line, stories and projects find it. Not this clears it, and that thread is never suggested for that observation again. Nothing joins a thread without a tap. When the model is busy or not reachable, no suggestion is made and nobody is told.

The director's reading, What these observations keep returning to, and Find threads both check the threads the team is following first. A pattern TREE matches to a followed thread, or one whose observations sit mostly in one followed thread, says This looks like the thread and offers to add the rest of its observations to it, so the evidence stays together instead of starting a second thread. The director can answer Not this thread, and the pattern can then be followed on its own. Find threads adds new evidence to followed threads and names them under its summary, and only threads that are new appear as found.

## Observations from Kaymbu

Observations shared from Kaymbu by email to the TREE address become TREE observations. Select the Observations in Kaymbu, send them to tree@childfamilycommunity.com, and put the class and the educator's first name in the subject line, such as South and a first name. Kaymbu is signed in per class, so the name in the subject is how TREE knows which educator shared the moments, matched to the educators who sign in to TREE. When an educator's first name is also a child's, write by before it, as in South by and a first name. A small group's name in the subject, such as Marigold, places the moments in that group's class, keeps the group on each observation, and credits the group's educator when no educator is named. Moments kept before this with only a small group in the subject are placed in that class once. A class newsletter, a Kaymbu Storyboard, can come to the TREE address too, sent by Kaymbu or forwarded by anyone at the school. Its title names the class, and each part under a heading becomes observations, one for each photo, with the heading and the words kept as written. A heading that names a small group, even spelled one letter off as Lavander, gives the part that group and credits the group's educator. A part with no group carries no educator, and whoever forwarded the newsletter is never credited. A photo already in TREE from its Moment keeps its own words and only takes the part's small group, if it has none, and a note that it was in the newsletter. The children named are offered on Today to be tagged, photo by photo. The calendar dates and reminders for families are left out. Moments kept before this take the educator from their subject once. The subject can also name a project or a thread, such as South Kindness. Each Observation's title and the educator's note are kept word for word, the photo is kept in the records at the largest size Kaymbu gives, and the observation is credited to the educator who shared it. The children are tagged by the names written in the note, the same way Tag the children named works on the page. When several photos arrive with the same title and note, the children named are offered on each photo instead, and the photo waits on Today as Check who is in a Kaymbu photo, so each photo carries only the children pictured in it. An observation where TREE could not tell the class or the children, where two children share a first name, where a name appears only in the title, or where a child's first name is also an everyday word, such as Sky, Hope or Joy, is not tagged outright. It waits on Today as Tag the children on a Kaymbu observation, for that class's educators and the director. Saving it with Change is the check. A Observation sent twice is only kept once. Kaymbu removes the camera's date from its photos, so each observation takes the day its photo reached Kaymbu, which is the day it was taken when the Kaymbu app is used in class. A date in the subject line, such as North 10/14 or North Oct 14, sets the day for the whole batch instead. The day can always be changed on the observation.

The email reaches TREE through a Google Apps Script in the school's Google Workspace mailbox, kept in tools/kaymbu-apps-script.gs with its setup steps. It checks every ten minutes and sends each new Kaymbu email to the server's Kaymbu inbox, which only accepts emails that came from Kaymbu and carry the key saved on Render as `KAYMBU_INBOX_KEY`. An email the server could not finish, such as a photo Kaymbu did not send in time, is tried again on the next check.

## Weekly bin

Plans, then Weekly bin. Each Class receives a standard FEED Sonoma bin, and on Tuesday a small group the Class team chooses opens it. The server reads the list FEED posts ahead of delivery at feedsonoma.com/weekly-bin, every six hours and whenever the page is opened, and keeps each week under bins/ in the records, so the year's record of what came grows by itself. A week is kept as FEED last listed it until the day it arrives. A list can also be added by hand when FEED's page cannot be read. From Saturday through Monday the page opens on the coming bin.

Each item shows its farm and the farm's town, and whether it came before. The towns come from FEED's producer list, checked against CCOF and the farms' own pages, and any educator can correct one under Where the farms are. The team keeps an idea bank of questions to hold and simple ways to prepare the food, which anyone can add to. Claude can suggest some of each from the week's produce and what the class is following, and a suggestion joins the idea bank only when an educator keeps it. Suggestions treat the food as food, prepared, tasted and shared, never as an art or play material, and someone eats it. Anything left goes in the chicken buckets for the chickens and goats at the end of the day, the goats only fruits and vegetables. Each Class chooses the ideas to use this week, and Print for the table prints them on the bin sheet with the produce and farms, as a PDF and a Word file. The class's own ideas, those it added or has used, come first, and ideas from other classes wait under From other classes with a small class tag. Under each idea in use, the page says which plan it is in, such as In North's Classroom plan, week of Oct 19, or Not in a plan yet. The bin sheet leaves out the amounts, and names read in small letters, Yellow onion. Keep an observation, kept from the bin page, keeps it with that week's bin, and it goes on to plans, threads and documentation like any other. From the day a bin arrives until six days later, the observation form anywhere in TREE shows a From the bin button, one tap to keep the observation with that bin and one more to undo. Observations from Kaymbu go with the bin when the word bin is in the subject line, such as South Bin. On Monday to Wednesday, Today shows the week's bin.

## Produce coming

Today and the Weekly bin show what the kitchen ordered for the week, for every educator, from the order confirmations kept for the grant, as a card named Produce coming. It opens on one line with the number of foods and farms and the day they come, and whether it is open is remembered on the device. The server gives only the food, its farm and the day it comes, never prices, totals, order numbers or the vendor, and leaves out bin lines and boxes, since the bin has its own page. Today shows the coming week from Saturday through Friday, with a link to the Weekly bin. The food is for lunch and snacks, so nothing suggests a separate tasting. Each food shows its farm and town and, when the menu uses it, the day and meal, such as Tue lunch, cheese pizza. Every snack comes with seasonal fruit and every lunch with a salad and seasonal vegetables, as the month's menu notes say, so a food with no named dish shows Fruit with snack, Salad with lunch or Side vegetable with lunch, and an onion or garlic shows Cooked into lunches this week. These are TREE's guess, in muted type. The director answers Side dish, Cooked in, or both, on each food, and the answer replaces the guess for everyone. It is kept on the order line and kept when the order is read again. Where the children met a food before is worked out but not shown for now. The Weekly bin page prints the week as a large type sheet for the classroom wall, This week our food came from.

Make a plan from this bin, on the Weekly bin page, starts an Individual plan or the class's Classroom plan for the bin's week and the class chosen on the page, and each piece of the bin goes to the section it belongs in. Observations kept with the bin, including Kaymbu observations with bin in the subject, go to the observation with their day and educator. - **Meetings**, one for each planning or group meeting, with the date, what Fulton brought, what others brought and the questions carried forward. For Leaving a Trace these are the bi-monthly CIRC meetings.
- **Evidence**. The server reads Fulton's own observations, plans, threads, projects and team inquiries, and the lens the entry reads through, for moments that fit the entry, by words anyone can check. Each shows the educators' words, the date, the Class and a line on why it fits, quoting the record. Leaving a Trace looks for traces that may not last, such as chalk, sand, water, light and shadow, arrangements, movement and song, and for traces that endure, a return, a reworking or a carrying on together with a mark or a structure. It reads through the lens Graphic Languages, which stays a lens. Serving Two-Year-Olds looks for moments with a child under three that day, by the children's birthdates, and in the Class that holds Fulton's two-year-olds. The director changes what TREE looks for under What TREE looks for. Keep adds a moment to Chosen moments, and Set aside moves it out of the way. Nothing comes from outside Fulton's records.
- **Chosen moments**, photos and educators' words chosen from Evidence, a lens or any observation, with the date, Class and language, dragged into order with a finger or a mouse, or moved up and down.
- **Readings and files**, from the shared library, a link with a short note, or a file from Google Drive added by pasting its Drive link. A licensing reference is taken only from CCLD or CDSS.
- **The plan**, built in sections, each with its minutes, what participants do, and the moments, readings and files it uses. Writing help drafts sections only from the notes, chosen moments and attached files, and nothing is added until chosen.
- **Self-paced follow-up**, optional, with short readings, reflection prompts and a brief check.
- **Prints**, the facilitator guide, panels with one idea to a page, a handout for participants and a slide set with large photos and words, each as PDF and Word. Children's names print as No names unless the director changes it for the entry.

A block on any PD day or monthly PD staff meeting agenda can open a Presenting entry, and the entry names the agendas that use it. Marking an entry presented logs its length as Fulton PD hours on each presenter's records page, lists it under What I led on their growth page, and offers to share it as a Proud Moment, where they choose who sees it. Marking it not presented takes those hours off again. A presented entry can be copied to start a new one, keeping the plan, moments, readings, files and follow-up. The entries are kept in the records under staff/presenting/, one file each, in presenting.js on the server and src/presenting-app.js on the page.


## Keeping records

Settings, then Keeping records, for the director. It sets how long TREE keeps the records of a child who has left Fulton and of a former educator, and names where each period comes from. Children's records are kept three years after a child leaves, from Title 22, section 101221(g), which says originals or photocopies of all children's records shall be kept for at least three years following termination of service to the child. Staff records are kept three years after an educator's last day, from Title 22, section 101217(d), which says all personnel records shall be retained for at least three years following termination of employment. Both are quoted on the card with a link to the Child Care Center regulations as the California Department of Social Services publishes them. A period TREE cannot confirm there is left blank for the director to set, and the card says so. The director can change either period.

When a former child or former educator reaches the period, the director's Today shows them once, under Records past their keeping period, with Keep longer and Remove. Nothing is removed until she chooses. Keep longer asks how many more years and asks again then. Remove asks once more before it happens. The server checks the period has passed, then removes the child's record and their conference records, or the former educator's records page, growth, journal, Proud Moments and sign-in, and writes the removal to the server log. Observations, plans and documentation stay as written. The removal, with the name, the day and who chose it, is kept in Settings, Keeping records, under Removed. The removed files stay in the records' history on GitHub and in the nightly backups made before. The choices are kept in settings/keeping in the records, which only the director's TREE receives.

## How it runs

- **Web page** `index.html`, published with GitHub Pages at https://renee-creator.github.io/fulton-planning/
- **Server** `server.js`, running on Render at https://fulton-planning.onrender.com. Open that address in a browser to see a status page.
- **Plans**, followed threads and projects are kept as files in the private repository `fulton-planning-records`. Every change is saved there with the educator's name, so earlier versions can always be recovered from GitHub's history.

This repository holds code only. It never holds plans, children's names, or keys.

## Nightly backup to Google Drive

Every night at 10 pm, Pacific time, the TREE mailbox's Apps Script asks the server for a backup. The server first writes any saves still waiting, then answers with one gzipped JSON file holding every record it keeps, plans, observations, threads, projects, settings, children, conferences, slides, inquiries, newsletters, bins, menus, the grant records, scans, the calendar's sign-ups and settings, and the DRDP descriptors. A manifest inside names the date, the number of records of each kind, the server version, and every photo by its id with its Google Drive id. Sign-ins, access/accounts.json, are left out because they hold passcodes. The director and office names, access/roles.json, are kept, and the educator records under staff/, the journal included. The script saves the file as TREE backup YYYY-MM-DD.json.gz in the TREE backups folder of the mailbox's Google Drive, keeps the newest 60, about two months, and moves older ones to the Drive trash. The Monday backups made before are named the same way, so they count among the 60 and leave in turn. The request goes to /inbound/backup with the same key as the other mailbox routes, and a sleeping server is asked again after it wakes. Settings shows the director a Backups card with the time the last backup arrived. Photos already live in Google Drive, and every record also keeps its full history in GitHub.

To set it up, or to change from the Monday backup, follow these steps.

1. In script.google.com, signed in as the TREE mailbox, open the TREE mailbox's Apps Script project.
2. Open the script file named weekly-backup, select everything in it, and paste tools/weekly-backup.gs over it. Without that file yet, choose the plus beside Files, then Script, name it weekly-backup and paste. Save.
3. Choose setupNightlyBackup in the toolbar and press Run, and allow the access Google asks for. It sets the nightly 10 pm time and removes the Monday time. setupWeeklyBackup, the name used before, does the same.
4. To test, run nightlyBackup and read the log. A new file appears in TREE backups, and the Backups card in Settings shows the time.

The web app needs no new deployment for the backup, since it does not change doPost. Until setup is run, the old Monday time still makes a backup each Monday, since weeklyBackup now runs the nightly one.

## Readable copies in Google Drive

The backup is for putting TREE back. Readable copies are for reading the school's records without TREE. Once a week, the first time the director has TREE open on a computer or iPad after Sunday night, her TREE quietly makes the Word and PDF files it already prints and keeps them in a folder called TREE readable copies in the TREE mailbox's Google Drive, beside TREE backups. A phone never makes them. Each file is made by the same print as its button, so it looks exactly as it does when printed, and it is handed back to TREE instead of saved on the device. Photos are drawn into the files as they are for printing.

Inside TREE readable copies is one folder for the week, named by the date the set was started, such as 2026-10-26. A second set started the same day is numbered, such as 2026-10-26 2. In it is a folder for each Class, North Class, South Class and West Class, one called School, one called Former children and one called Former educators. A Class folder holds each child's portfolio, this school year's plans, one file for each week as the week's plans download makes it, the Class's threads, the projects whose home is the Class, its team inquiries, the conference forms of its children, and the records page of each educator in the Class. Projects and threads with no home Class, team inquiries for the whole school, and the records pages of people with no Class, the office and the kitchen among them, go in School. Children who have left, with status withdrawn, keep their portfolios and conference forms in Former children. Educators whose last day has passed, by leaveOn on their sign-in or leftOn on their record, have their records page in Former educators. Projects and team inquiries are the full prints, and conference forms are the PDF and Word file the conference form makes, with its documentation. A child with nothing in the portfolio yet has no portfolio file. Evaluations, journals, check-ins, the Growth Plan and its reflection, anything else kept only for the director, and passcodes are never made into these files.

The files go one at a time, each in pieces of no more than 4 MB, through the server's /api/readable, which only the director's sign-in may use, to the same mailbox web app that keeps TREE's photos (DRIVE_PHOTOS_URL with KAYMBU_INBOX_KEY). Only one device makes a set at a time. A set that stops partway, because TREE was closed or the connection dropped, carries on the next time TREE opens, in the same week's folder, and files already there are not sent again. Only once every file of the new set has arrived does the mailbox script move earlier weeks' folders to the Drive trash, so the newest complete set is always kept and only one is kept. Settings shows the director, under Backups, when the last complete set was made, how many files it holds, a set in progress, a set that did not finish, and a week that was missed. Make readable copies now makes a set at once on a computer or iPad, and it replaces the week's set.

To set it up, the TREE mailbox's Apps Script project needs a new script file and one new line in doPost, and the web app is deployed again.

1. In script.google.com, signed in as the TREE mailbox, open the TREE mailbox's Apps Script project.
2. Open the Kaymbu inbox script file and find function doPost. Right below its line `if (!body.key || body.key !== TREE_KEY) return reply_({ error: 'Not allowed' });` add the line `if (/^readable-/.test(String(body.op || ''))) return reply_(readableCopies_(body));` and save. The doPost in tools/kaymbu-apps-script.gs already has it, so pasting that whole function over the old one does the same.
3. Choose the plus beside Files, then Script, name it readable-copies, paste tools/readable-copies.gs into it, and save.
4. Choose Deploy, then Manage deployments. Choose the web app, then the pencil to edit it. Under Version choose New version, and press Deploy. The address stays the same, so DRIVE_PHOTOS_URL on Render does not change.
5. Choose setupReadableCopies in the toolbar and press Run, and allow the access Google asks for. It makes the TREE readable copies folder and names it in the log.
6. To test, open TREE on a computer as the director, go to Settings, and under Backups press Make readable copies now. Keep TREE open until the card shows the time of the new set, then open TREE readable copies in Drive.

## Restore

1. Download the backup you want from TREE backups in the mailbox's Google Drive.
2. Clone the records repository, or start an empty folder for a new one.
3. Run node tools/restore-backup.js "TREE backup 2026-10-19.json.gz" path/to/records. Each record is written back as its own file, at the path it had, in the format the server writes.
4. Commit and push the folder to the records repository, and restart the service on Render so it reads them.
5. Sign-ins are not in a backup. If they were lost too, the director adds the educators again in Settings, Sign-ins, and each signs in the first time with the six number start code shown beside their name, then chooses a passcode.

Photos are not inside the backup. Those moved to Google Drive stay in the TREE photos folder, and the markers that point TREE to them, photos/*.ref, are listed in the manifest with their Drive ids. Photos still in the records come back with the records repository itself.

## Who can do what

A new sign-in starts with a six number start code, shown only to the director beside the name on Sign-ins. The educator types it the first time they sign in, then chooses their own four numbers, and the server refuses a first sign-in without it. A cleared code gives a new start code.

Any educator can stop following a thread, which moves it to No longer following, and anyone can follow it again. A thread followed by mistake can be undone, which puts it back among the threads Claude found as if it had never been followed. Any educator can connect a plan to a thread the school is following, or disconnect it. The reading and the intent can be revised, and earlier versions are kept with their dates. A project holds possible directions as hypotheses and has no end date or steps to complete. A direction is named with a short label and its materials sit under it, and the drawing sets text smaller until it fits rather than dropping the end of a line. Claude never starts a project, adds a thread to one, or writes the team's reading of what may lie underneath. It points out a new thread that may be related. On the project page it offers the same two kinds of help a plan has. Strengthen wording rewords what educators wrote and adds nothing, and the possible directions can be drafted from what the project and its threads already say. Each suggestion is shown first and nothing changes until an educator chooses it. Every kind of plan that follows a thread stays connected to it. The plan offers the thread's observations first, from any class, with the class's other observations one tap away, and the note at the top shows the thread's question, the weeks it has been seen and its latest evidence. A followed thread can be brought into the Classroom Plan for the week. Educators see it read-only, are told in the sidebar when a note is waiting, and can reply under it. Everyone can open the Lens page. Only the director can run Find threads with Claude, ask Claude to read through the lens, change the lens wording in Program settings, change Program settings, remove a thread permanently, and remove a project. A photo or a kept file opens through an address that carries a key for that one photo, good for twelve hours. The Family page and the staff room display get each photo's key from the server. A child's Class changes only through the director, in Move children, and the server keeps the Class as it was when anyone else saves a child, from Conferences or anywhere else. Conference notes reach a family only when the director or the office shares them, and the server keeps the sharing as it was when an educator saves a conference.

## Server notes

The TREE server is on a paid Render plan and never sleeps. If the connection drops for a moment, TREE keeps the words on the device and sends them once it is back, as Drafts on the phone describes. Typing is gathered and saved to GitHub within about 15 seconds.

The wall keeps up with its project. It opens on the whole school, a few sentences on what is followed, how much sits in a thread, how much TREE connected on its own and what is not connected yet, then a map of every class with its threads, each thread edged in the colour of its project and dashed where TREE found it. Below, each class opens to everything in each of its threads and to its plans and observations not connected yet, followed by what reaches across classes and the moments children from different classes shared. Tapping a thread or a project opens its map and everything in it, and Back returns to the whole school. All plans is the plan list again.

## Conference status and SignUpGenius

A conference shows Ready once the thirteen sections an educator prepares have writing and the four preparation steps are checked, so the list, the class cards and the child's page show it at a glance. Complete, Did not sign up and Date passed still come first. Every hour the server reads the SignUpGenius calendar link from the conference Settings, matches each booked slot to a child by the child's or a parent's name, and fills in the date, the time and Signed up. A slot that matches no child or more than one, or a child with two slots, waits for Fill in times from SignUpGenius. A date an educator set by hand is never changed. A family who moves their slot in SignUpGenius is followed. Setting signupAuto to false in the main settings stops the hourly check.

## Sending a plan to the director

Every box on the Individual and Classroom plans needs writing before the plan is sent, except Final remarks, which is written after the week. Send to the director sits at the end of the plan. Tapped with boxes still empty, it names them, each a link to its box, and opens a saved plan to edit. Once the last box has writing and the educator pauses, TREE asks once whether to send it. Leaving a draft with empty boxes brings a short note saying how many are left, and never stops anyone leaving. A sent plan shows as Waiting for the director's review, and only the director marks it Reviewed or sends it back to draft, which she can do while reading it. The server keeps the same rule, so a plan is never sent with a box empty. The director's Today lists every plan waiting for her review, and each school morning she hears how many are waiting. An educator hears a few minutes after the director marks a plan reviewed or leaves a note, and sees it on Today until the plan is opened. From Tuesday, an educator whose plan is written but still a draft is reminded to send it. The older Small Group plan keeps its own status row.

## Drafts on the phone

While an educator writes an observation or a plan, TREE keeps the words and any photo on that device, in the browser's own store, which holds photos that the smaller page storage could not. Drafts are kept under the educator signed in, so another educator signing in on a shared phone never sees them. If a save fails because the signal dropped, nothing is lost. A quiet note under the header, and on the observation's card, reads Saved on this phone, it will send when you are connected, and the draft sends by itself when the connection comes back, when TREE opens and when it comes back into view. Drafts go one at a time, and a tab holding a draft keeps another tab from sending it too. An observation being written comes back as it was left, photos and all, if TREE is closed before Keep.

Each save from a draft carries a client id made once, and each photo one made from its observation's, so a save sent again after its answer was lost on the way back is the same save. The server hands back what it already kept instead of keeping it twice, so a retry never makes a second observation or a second copy of a photo. A draft is removed only once it is sent. If an educator signs out with something unsent, TREE says so first, with Stay signed in and Sign out anyway. Signing out keeps the draft on that phone, and it sends when she signs in there again.

## Saves are never lost

Every change to the records goes through one queue on the server, one at a time, with a pause of about a second between changes as GitHub asks. GitHub makes each change a commit on the same branch, and two commits made in the same moment refuse each other. Before October 24, 2026 each file was saved on its own, so a dozen saves arriving together, such as small groups set on many observations at once or a whole Class of conference forms, refused each other and the log read GitHub kept refusing the save. A refused save is now tried again on the newest version of the file, up to eight times with a growing pause, and after that every 30 seconds while the server runs.

Every save from a device carries a save id, and the commit that writes it names the id in its message. The device keeps its own copy of each save, in the browser's own store, until the server says it is written to the records. A save the server could not take shows on the device at once and waits under the same quiet note as drafts, Saved on this phone, it will send when you are connected. A save the server took but never wrote, such as one waiting when the server was updated, is found missing from the records' history by its id, and the device sends it again, with any later saves to the same record after it, in the order they were made. The server never makes the same save twice. A save the server refuses for a reason, such as one only the director may make, is let go and says why. The Conferences page inside TREE saves the same way. Signing out with a save not yet written says so first.

When a save needed more than three tries, the director's Today shows it once a day under Saves that needed several tries, with the record's name, kept in settings/saves for 30 days. Seen keeps it away until the next day.

## The evidence beside a plan

The evidence panel shows what the plan is built on first. That is the project, threads and inquiries it follows, last week's Final remarks, and for an Individual plan last week's Classroom plan for the class. Below them come the observations attached to the plan or kept with what it follows, and from the last three weeks the observations of the children in its small groups and the class's whole-class moments, kept with no child named or with half the class or more named. ## Search

Search, at the top of every page, looks across every observation, photo, plan, thread, project, slide, wall and story. It finds words, children's names and educators, and narrows by child, small group and when, this month, this school year or last. Photos are found by their words and the children tagged in them. Results are grouped by kind with the newest first, and each opens where it lives, with Back to Search.

## Proofread observations

Observations, then Proofread observations, for the director only, with the number still left on the button. The page gathers every saved observation never proofread, oldest first, and reads four at a time with the same proofread TREE runs before a save, a few ahead of the director, with a bar showing how far it has come. Photos of one post with the same words are proofread once, together. Each observation shows what was written beside the proofread words, with what was taken out and what was put in marked. Proofreading fixes spelling, grammar and punctuation only. It never changes meaning or adds or takes away what the educator observed, and keeps children's names, children's quoted words and Class words as written. TREE leaves out any fix that would change one of those, or add or remove more than a few words.

The director can tap Approve all, approve or skip any one, or edit the proofread words before approving. Nothing is saved until she approves. An approved observation is saved as proofread, with the educator who kept it still its author, and the words it had stay in the records' history on GitHub. A skipped one stays in the list for next time. When the proofread cannot be reached, the page says so in a plain sentence, keeps the rest of the list, and Try again goes on from there.

## Transition summary

A child marked Going to kindergarten next year or Going to TK next year has a Transition summary tab on their page, beside Conferences. It is one to two pages for the family and the next school, in five parts, Who this child is, How they play and learn, Friends and relationships, What they are working on, and What helps, with the child's age in years and months. Draft the summary asks TREE's writing help to draft it only from the child's own observations, the slides in the child's portfolio and the child's conference notes, keeping the educators' words. An observation taken out of the portfolio or kept for a team inquiry is never read, the conference form's DRDP evidence box is left out, and other children are named by initial, as in the portfolio. The draft adds nothing the educators did not write. A sentence that names the DRDP, a level, a score or a rating, or uses words such as on track, ahead of or delayed, is left out of the draft, and a part an educator writes with those words says so and cannot be marked ready. An empty part takes the draft, and a part already written keeps its words with the draft waiting beside it to use or set aside.

Educators change any part, proofread it with the same Proofread as every page, and mark it Ready, and Mark ready proofreads first. A change after it is ready sends it back to draft. It prints as a PDF and a Word file. The summary is kept on the child's record for the school year, saved part by part so two educators keep each other's words, and it is in the nightly backup with the rest of the child's record.

It reaches the family only when the director shares it, from the child's Details tab under Shared with the family, once it is ready. The family sees it on the Family page as it read when she shared it, and a change made after that waits until she shares it again. She can stop sharing it at any time. The server keeps the rule, so an educator's or the office's save never shares a summary or takes one away, a whole child record saved from anywhere keeps the summary as it is, and the Family page receives nothing of a summary that is not shared.

## The Family page

family.html is the Family page, one link for every family. A parent signs in with the email the school has for them and a six-digit code, and sees only their own children. They find ways to take part, sign up, log what they did once it is done, choose which child it counts for, and see each child's hours toward the year's goal of 10. Each entry rounds up to the next whole hour. Signing up adds no hours. Records live under participation/, seen and changed only by the director and the office, anyone named in access/roles.json in the private records or in OFFICE_NAMES. Children, then Participation, is where they post tasks and community events, see every child's hours with the furthest behind first, and remove an entry that is not right.

The Family Handbook opens on the page itself, every part folded until opened, with earlier editions one tap away. A family new to Fulton, or re-enrolling, says they have read the current edition in their first steps.

Test mode is on until the director turns it off. While it is on, only the testers listed on the Participation page sign in, each linked to real children, the code shows on the page, nothing is emailed, educators do not see the page, and everything made is marked Test and cleared with one button. Live sign-in codes are emailed through the school's Google Workspace mailbox script, set on Render as FAMILY_MAIL_URL and FAMILY_MAIL_KEY.

public.html is the public menu page for the website, with no sign-in. It shows the month's menu and the month before's Farm to School story, the farms and producers, how many are in Sonoma County and how far the food travelled, with the grant's funding line.
